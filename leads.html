What I do differently, and why it works.

Most people write first and ask questions later. I do it the other way around, in four steps, and that is why the message reads like it was written for that company. Because it was.

1 · Research before a single word

Your market and your competitors, read in Colombia's mercantile registry, public contracting and open data before I write anything. Which companies are alive, what they just did, and why now.

2 · What moves your buyer

What they already know about their problem, what it costs them, and what makes them act. The message enters through there, not through your product.

3 · Proven structures

AIDA (attention, interest, desire, action) and PAS (problem, agitation, solution). Each message makes one point and asks for one thing.

4 · Decisions with numbers

Every week: sent, replies, meetings, each with its denominator. The next step comes out of those numbers, not out of a hunch.

BEFORE YOU ASK

The questions I get before we start.

Which domain does this go out from?

A dedicated mailbox, warmed up beforehand, on a secondary domain. Never your main domain. Your reputation is not touched.

Do you touch my LinkedIn account?

No. I write every message; on your account, you press send. No program ever enters your LinkedIn.

Is this legal?

Yes. Inside Ley 2300 de 2023: institutional mailboxes, permitted hours, frequency and opt-out as the law sets them, never cold WhatsApp. And Ley 1581 on personal data.

Is it you or an agency?

It is me. I write, I send, I handle every reply. Nobody hands you to a junior.

I already have a database. Why this?

A database tells you who exists. The cut tells you who to write to, why, and why now, with what backs it: a renewed registration, a state contract, active ads. The message that goes out with that reason is the work.
